Signal trains in a writable sea

Same connection. Different moment. Different machine.

A signal has to do two different things: reach an island, and arrive while that island is receptive. Gelatin Island keeps those causes separate, then lets a poke tell them apart.

Three clocks

FAST / MEDIUM / SLOW

FAST
Train arrival + receiver phase.
A few milliseconds can change whether an otherwise identical arrival has leverage.
MEDIUM
Island integration state.
Local geometry and dynamics determine how long recent inputs remain able to cooperate.
SLOW
Material substrate.
Repeated traffic can deposit a route trace; the world itself changes the propagation operator seen by later trains.
history → material → route → arrival timing × primer → local integration → launch → new train → material

What inspired each piece

Arrival phase

Drebitz, Rausch & Kreiter (2025): electrically evoked V2 volleys had strongly gamma-phase-dependent effects downstream in V4 and behavior.

10.1038/s41467-025-62732-8

Integration windows

Norman-Haignere et al. (2025): auditory cortical integration windows grow across hierarchy and are predominantly yoked to absolute time.

10.1038/s41593-025-02060-8

Launcher boundary

Leterrier (2018): the axon initial segment generates/shapes action potentials, separates compartments, and is structurally plastic.

10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1922-17.2018

Spatial island

Aizenbud et al. (2026): dendritic morphology, compartmentalization and nonlinear synapses contribute to modeled neuronal I/O complexity.

10.1073/pnas.2533168123
